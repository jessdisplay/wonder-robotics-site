Before we sold a robot kitchen to anyone, we built one for ourselves and opened it to the public. A converted warehouse in the Valley with a kitchen that fries, boils and plates from an ingredient store, a bar where an arm pours to order, a dessert kiosk that runs unattended, and robots carrying plates across a real dining floor.

Everything on this page is the building as it is. Photographed in it, not rendered.

The kitchen

A six-axis cobot on a linear rail works the whole line: an ingredient store behind it, fryers and a row of noodle baths in front, the pass to the dining room at the end. Orders arrive from the screens and the app; the arm does the rest.

The bar

A cream curved counter with a cobot behind it and the bottles overhead. Coffee, cocktails and soft drinks, ordered at the screen and poured to order, with a person on the floor rather than behind the bar.

The kiosk

Soft serve and slushies from a kiosk that runs itself: a screen to order, an arm to pour, payment built in. It's the smallest thing in the building and the one most people film.

The floor

Two classes of service robot work the room: CadeBots carrying plates on mapped routes between the pass and the tables, and a Cruzr greeting at the door. The routes, the integration with ordering and the staff training are ours.

What we did

All five steps, on our own account: scope, design, brand, build and run. The building is where clients come to see the machines work before they buy them, and where we test what we install elsewhere.
