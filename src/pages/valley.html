The brief

Our own. Before we sold a robot kitchen to anyone, build one for ourselves and open it to the public, so the proof is a lunch service and not a slide. A converted warehouse at 365 St Pauls Terrace in the Valley: a kitchen, a bar, a dessert kiosk and a dining floor, all of it run by machines, all of it open six days.

Planning

Nobody buys a robot kitchen from a brochure. They buy it after watching one cook their lunch, and after asking what broke last week. The building is the top of the funnel for everything else we do: clients come here to see the machines work before they buy them, and everything we install elsewhere gets run here first.

Design

The kitchen took the long wall, with the ingredient store behind the rail and the pass at the end so the room can watch the whole line. The bar is a cream curved counter with the cobot behind it and the bottles overhead. The kiosk sits where the queue forms. The floor is mapped for the CadeBots, pass to tables and back, with a Cruzr at the door.

The brand pass is Wonder itself: the crescent, the cream and the night, the mark on the screens and on the door, all drawn here.

Supply

A six-axis cobot on a linear rail with an ingredient store, three fryer stations and a row of six noodle baths. A bar cobot with dispensers. A kiosk arm with soft serve and slushies. Two UBTECH CadeBots and a Cruzr on the floor. The ordering, payment and stock stack behind all of it, ours.

Implementation

The kitchen: orders arrive from the screens and the app, the arm picks from the store, fries, boils and plates, and the bowl goes out on the pass. The bar: coffee, cocktails and soft drinks ordered at the screen and poured to order, with a person on the floor rather than behind the bar. The kiosk: a screen to order, an arm to pour, payment built in, the smallest thing in the building and the one most people film. The floor: CadeBots on mapped routes between the pass and the tables, the routes, the integration with ordering and the staff training all ours.

Maintenance

We run it six days a week, so we are our own first service call. What breaks here gets fixed here and then designed out of the next install. Once a month the Robotics and Hardware Club has the building for a night and the bench is open to anyone who wants to poke at the machines.

The outcome

Open six days. The kitchen cooks the full menu with nobody at the pass, the bar pours to order, the kiosk runs unattended, and Pepper has been on the door long enough to have favourites. Everything on this page is the building as it is, photographed in it, not rendered.
