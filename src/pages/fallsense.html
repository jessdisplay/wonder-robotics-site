Most of our work starts with somebody else's building. This one started on a bench here: a shallow disc that mounts on the ceiling of a care home bedroom and works out what is happening below it without ever making a picture.

Florence is its own company with its own name, palette and type, built here alongside the device. Fall Sense is the first thing it sells. There is no arm in it and nothing moves.

The brief

A resident goes down at two in the morning. The call button is on the wall by the bed and they are on the floor by the bathroom, so it may as well be in another building. Nobody finds out until the round. The brief, written on our own bench: know that it happened, the minute it happened, without a camera in the room.

Planning

The obvious fix is a camera in the bedroom, and every operator who has tried it has run into the same wall. Residents will not have it, families will not have it, and they are right. A bedroom is somebody's home.

So the unit works out what is happening below it without ever producing a picture a person could look at. What leaves it is the event: somebody is on the floor, somebody has been in the bathroom for forty minutes, somebody has not got out of bed this morning. That is the whole design argument. There is no footage to leak, no screen for a night staffer to sit in front of, and nothing for a family to object to on a tour. It also needed its own name, because a care home will not put a robotics company's logo on a bedroom ceiling. Florence is that company.

Design

It started as a speaker. Every reference on the board was a round thing that recesses into a ceiling and shows a mesh face, so the first page of the book is a dozen of those, most of them crossed out. What survived: a bezel no prouder than a downlight, a domed face, and the ring of light tried at four widths before it settled at the thinnest one.

The brand pass. Florence is a separate company with its own name, palette and type, and nothing in it borrows from Wonder: a halo drawn in perspective for the mark, a humanist serif, warm stone and clay with no blue anywhere. The room it has to earn its place in is the reason.

Supply

Seven parts. Bezel, mesh face, the diffuser ring that carries the light, the board with the sensor module at its centre, a spreader plate, the can, and two spring clips. Edge compute on the board, so the model runs in the room. Python and Postgres behind it, and the same dashboard our kitchen managers use, because we had already written it.

Implementation

It pushes up into a 90 mm cutout and the clips hold it, which is the same install as a downlight, so an electrician fits it in the time it takes to fit a downlight. One to a room. Then the only thing that crosses the ceiling is a sentence.

Maintenance

Firmware updates over the air. Every event logged with the time. When a unit stops reporting, it tells us before it tells you, and the line that says so is on the same dashboard as the falls.
