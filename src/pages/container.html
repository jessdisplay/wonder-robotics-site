The containerkitchen

Site
Eat Street Northshore, Hamilton, Brisbane
Format
Twenty foot shipping container, one side opening to a serving hatch
Machine
Six-axis arm on a rail, cooking line behind glass
Skin
Micro-LED wrap across three faces of the box
Scope
Planning, design, brand, fabrication, implementation, maintenance
Status
In development

Eat Street is 180 repurposed shipping containers on the river at Hamilton, more than seventy of them kitchens, open Friday to Sunday from four in the afternoon. Every one of them is fighting for the same eyes. A robot kitchen has an advantage there. People stop to watch it work.

So the box does two jobs. The arm cooks behind glass, and the skin of the container is a screen.

The brief

Our own. A twenty foot container kitchen for Eat Street Northshore in Hamilton, where 180 containers trade Friday to Sunday and more than seventy of them are kitchens. It has to cook without a brigade, and it has to win the three seconds someone gives a stall as they walk past with a drink in their hand.

Planning

Signage does not win three seconds, and neither does a menu board, because everyone has one and they all look the same after the fourth container. A robot arm working behind glass does. People stop to watch it.

The second problem is the box. A twenty foot container is 6 metres by 2.4, and a commercial kitchen wants more than that. Put a brigade in there and you lose the floor to people. Put an arm in there and the people leave, the cooking stays, and the window you would have filled with staff becomes the show. The container is ours, so it is also the prototype for the next client who asks for one.

Design

One long side comes out for the hatch, which folds up to become the awning. The arm runs on a rail down the back wall over a fryer and a hot line, extraction goes out through the roof, and the counter runs the length of the opening.

The brand pass is the skin. Three faces of the container carry an emissive micro-LED wrap. It is not a printed vinyl and it is not a television bolted to the side. The panel is the cladding, so the corrugation reads through it and the whole box lights up. That buys a thing paint cannot: the container is one brand on Friday and another on Saturday. Run the Wonder crescent when it is ours, run a rainforest when the menu turns, run a sponsor when someone has paid for the night.

Supply

A twenty foot high-cube container. A six-axis arm on a horizontal rail, a fryer, a hot line, extraction, the glass screen and the hatch hardware. Micro-LED panels for three faces and the controller that drives them. Behind the counter, the same stack that runs the kitchen at 365 St Pauls Terrace: ordering, payment, stock, the manager dashboard, and the content scheduler for the wrap.

Implementation

The box arrives as a box. One long side comes out for the hatch and gets reframed in steel, the shell is lined and insulated, then the stainless goes in and the arm goes on its rail. Every stage happens in our yard, so the first time it meets the public it has already cooked.

Maintenance

It trades three nights and sits in our yard the other four, which is the easiest service schedule we have. The arm, the fryer and the wrap all report to the same dashboard as our kitchen, and the content for the skin is scheduled from the office, not from a ladder.

The outcome

In development. The first trading weekend, what the wrap did to the queue against the container next door, and the photography go here once it is on site.
