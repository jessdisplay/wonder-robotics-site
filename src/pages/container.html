The containerkitchen

Site
Eat Street Northshore, Hamilton, Brisbane
Format
Twenty foot shipping container, one side opening to a serving hatch
Machine
Six-axis arm on a rail, cooking line behind glass
Skin
Micro-LED wrap across three faces of the box
Scope
Planning, design, brand, fabrication, implementation, maintenance
Status
In development

Eat Street is 180 repurposed shipping containers on the river at Hamilton, more than seventy of them kitchens, open Friday to Sunday from four in the afternoon. Every one of them is fighting for the same eyes. A robot kitchen has an advantage there. People stop to watch it work.

So the box does two jobs. The arm cooks behind glass, and the skin of the container is a screen.

The problem

A market stall gets about three seconds. Someone walks past with a drink in their hand and decides. Signage does not win that, and neither does a menu board, because everyone has one and they all look the same after the fourth container.

The second problem is the box itself. A twenty foot container is 6 metres by 2.4, and a commercial kitchen wants more than that. Put a brigade in there and you lose the floor to people. Put an arm in there and the people leave, the cooking stays, and the window you would have filled with staff becomes the show.

Scope

Concept, the room drawn to build, the brand and the screen content, the fabrication, the arm and its integration, the software, commissioning, then the maintenance contract. Every stage in one building. The container is our own, so this one is also the prototype for the next client who asks for a version of it.

How it is built

The box arrives as a box. One long side comes out for the hatch and gets reframed in steel, the shell is lined and insulated, then the stainless goes in and the arm goes on its rail. Every stage happens in our yard, so the first time it meets the public it has already cooked.

Why the skin is a screen

Three faces of the container carry an emissive micro-LED wrap. It is not a printed vinyl and it is not a television bolted to the side. The panel is the cladding, so the corrugation reads through it and the whole box lights up.

That buys a thing paint cannot. The container is one brand on Friday and another on Saturday. Run the Wonder crescent when it is ours, run a rainforest when the menu turns, run a sponsor when someone has paid for the night. Same steel, different business.

What is in it

A six-axis arm on a horizontal rail down the back wall, working a fryer and a hot line. Extraction out through the roof. The hatch folds up to become the awning, and the counter runs the length of it. Behind the counter, the same stack that runs the kitchen at 365 St Pauls Terrace: ordering, payment, stock, the manager dashboard, and the content scheduler that drives the wrap.
