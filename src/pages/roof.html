Most of our work starts with somebody else's building. This one started on a bench here: a small weatherproof box that sits on a roof, watches the yard underneath it, and works out what is happening without a person watching a screen.

It is the reason the software column exists on this site. There is no arm in it and nothing moves.

The problem

Every yard, car park, loading dock and site compound in the country already has cameras on it. Almost none of them are being watched. The footage is there so somebody can go back and look after the thing has happened, which is the least useful moment to find out.

The other half of the problem is the install. Running power and data to a roof is the expensive part of any camera job, and drilling a roof sheet to mount something is how a building gets a leak.

What it does about it

It runs the model on the device. The vision stays inside the box and what comes out is the answer: how many, where, how long, and whether that is unusual for a Tuesday. No video leaves the roof unless somebody asks for it, which is cheaper to run and a much shorter conversation about privacy.

The bracket clamps to a purlin so nothing is drilled through the sheet, and the solar face means one person on a ladder finishes the job.

The stack

Sealed enclosure to IP66, cut and folded in the workshop here. The optics sit under a smoked dome so the unit gives away nothing about where it is pointed. Edge compute on the board, models trained on our own footage from 365 St Pauls Terrace before they go anywhere near a client site. Python and Postgres behind it, and the same dashboard our kitchen managers use, because we had already written it.

Firmware updates over the air. When one stops reporting, it tells us before it tells you.
